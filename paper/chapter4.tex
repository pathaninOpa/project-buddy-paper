\chapter{RESULT}
\hspace{\parindent}
This chapter presents the experimental results obtained from the evaluation of the “Buddy” interactive companion system. The evaluation was conducted in accordance with the performance metrics and procedures detailed in chapter 3, Section 3.6, and modified to incorporate several evaluation techniques.
\par
The primary focus of this chapter is to quantify the performance of the core components of the “Buddy” system:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Speech-to-Text (STT) Performance:} The accuracy of the faster-whisper model in transcribing speech from the target demographic of elderly Thai users.
	\item \textbf{Emotion Recognition Performance:} The effectiveness of the Language Model (Gemma 3:4b) in classifying the user's emotion from the transcribed text.
	\item \textbf{Dialogue Generation Quality:} The coherence, fluency, and appropriateness of the companion’s generated responses, as measured by both human survey scores and an “LLM-as-Judge” methodology
	\item \textbf{Video Call (Telecommunication) Performance:} The real-world quality and stability of the WebRTC video communication link.
	\item \textbf{Application UI and User Satisfaction:} The usability and perceived satisfaction of the caretaker-facing mobile application.
\end{itemize}
\par
The results are presented through a combination of quantitative metrics and qualitative analysis to provide a comprehensive understanding of the system’s strengths and weaknesses.

\section{Speech-to-Text (STT) Performance}
\noindent\hspace{2.2em}The STT subsystem, utilizing the faster-whisper model, serves as the primary input for the entire interaction pipeline. Its accuracy is therefore critical to the system's overall function, especially given the project's focus on elderly Thai speakers, who may have unique speech patterns, accents, and vocabulary. Performance was evaluated using a publicly available test dataset of elderly Thai speech assessing both Word Error Rate (WER) and Character Error Rate (CER) as defined in Section 3.6.3.

\section{Emotion Recognition Performance}
\noindent\hspace{2.2em}Following the transcription, GEMMA 3:4b model was tasked with performing text based emotion classification. The model was evaluated against a manually labeled test set using the metrics outlined in Section 3.6.1. The performance is summarized by the confusion matrix (Figure 4.1) and the per-class metrics (Table 4.2).

\section{Dialogue Generation Quality}
\noindent\hspace{2.2em}The quality of the HRI (Human Robot Interaction) was evaluated by assessing the dialog generated by the GEMMA 3:4b model, using a human-based survey score and an “LLM-as-Judge” evaluation.

\subsection{Human Evaluation (Survey Score)}
\noindent\hspace{2.2em}A user study involving 15 participants from the target demographic was conducted. Participants interacted with the “Buddy” system and subsequently rated the quality of its response using a 5-point Mean Opinion Score (MOS) from scale (1=Bad, 5=Excellent). The relevance metrics are Coherence, Fluency, Empathy \& Appropriateness, Overall Quality.

\subsection{LLM-as-Judge Evaluation}
\noindent\hspace{2.2em}To complement the human survey and provide a scalable evaluation, a superior large language model (e.g., GPT-oss or GEMMA 3:27b-it-qat) was employed as a “judge.” The judge was provided with the user’s transcribed input, the detected emotion, and the "Buddy" (GEMMA 3:4b) response. It was then prompted to score the response on a scale of 1-5 across several key criteria.

\section{Telecommunication system}
\noindent\hspace{2.2em}In the real world situation, the monitoring type app needs to have as much consistency as it could have, especially one that involves the elderly. This section will demonstrate the evaluation on the performance of the telecommunication system. There are 3 factors that need to be considered about
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{Connection success rate}: The percentage of the success attempt over the total number of attempts that occur between two devices. 
	\item \textbf{Latency}: The amount of time that two devices need to use for communication.
	\item \textbf{Quality of the call}: The satisfaction that the telecom system can provide to the user.. MOS score, User experience rating
\end{itemize}

\noindent\hspace{2.2em}The effectiveness of the system also depends on the usability of the caretaker mobile application. A user satisfaction survey was conducted with [N] test users (acting as caretakers) after they performed a set of standardized tasks (e.g., initiating a call, checking an emotion report, viewing the live feed).
\par
